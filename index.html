About

I am a PhD researcher in the Department of Bioengineering at Imperial College London. My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence and medical imaging, spanning X-ray, MRI, and PET. I am particularly interested in generative AI, vision-language models, medical image reconstruction and synthesis, and clinically grounded learning and reasoning.

My research explores how AI systems can learn from and reason about complex medical imaging data while remaining robust, interpretable, and clinically meaningful. My work ranges from generative approaches for image reconstruction, restoration, and cross-modality synthesis to vision-language models and large language model training for radiological understanding and report generation.

I am supervised by Dr. Guang Yang , Associate Professor (Reader) at Imperial College London, where I am a member of YangLab; Dr. Javier A. Montoya-Zegarra , Professor at the ZHAW School of Engineering ; and Dr. Angelica Aviles-Rivero , Assistant Professor at the Yau Mathematical Sciences Center , Tsinghua University , where she leads MathMLX.

My PhD research is supported by the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) under Grant No. 20HW-1 220785.

Research

Low-Dose PET Imaging

Developing deep-learning methods for ultra-low-dose PET restoration and reconstruction while preserving quantitative radiotracer uptake and clinically relevant lesion information.

Generative Medical Imaging

Investigating diffusion models and other generative approaches for image reconstruction, denoising, enhancement, and cross-modality synthesis.

Robust & Clinical AI

Studying reliability, uncertainty, lesion-level evaluation, segmentation, and domain robustness for medical imaging AI systems intended for clinical use.

MAP-Diff: Multi-Anchor Guided Diffusion for Progressive 3D Whole-Body Low-Dose PET Denoising

Peiyuan Jing, Chun-Wun Cheng, Liutao Yang, ··· Guang Yang, Javier A. Montoya-Zegarra

MICCAI 2026 — Oral Presentation

A multi-anchor guided diffusion framework that constrains the reverse denoising trajectory using clinically observed intermediate-dose PET scans, enabling progressive and dose-consistent whole-body PET restoration.

npj Artificial Intelligence, 2026

Gaze-X incorporates radiologists' eye-tracking behaviour into vision-language model pretraining, enabling the model to learn expert-like spatial and temporal diagnostic attention patterns.
